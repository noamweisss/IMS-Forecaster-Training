Definition
Atmospheric stability describes a parcel's tendency to return to its original altitude after a small vertical displacement. A stable parcel resists displacement; an unstable parcel accelerates away from its starting point. The diagnostic compares the parcel's adiabatic cooling to the surrounding environmental lapse rate.

Operational caveat
Stability assessment from a single sounding is one snapshot in time and space. Diurnal heating, mesoscale boundaries, and frontal passages all modify the profile within hours. Always cross-check the model forecast and the next sounding.
Key relation
Parcel cooler than environment → stable. Parcel warmer than environment → unstable. The crossing point on the diagram is the level of free convection (LFC).
Danger condition
When ELR exceeds DALR through a deep layer with surface moisture > 12 g/kg, expect explosive convective development. Issue a SIGMET well before initiation, not after.

Key Takeaways

  • Stability is the parcel's response to vertical displacement, not a property of the air itself.
  • Three lapse rates matter operationally: DALR (9.8 °C/km), SALR (~6 °C/km), and the measured ELR.
  • When ELR > DALR through a layer, that layer is absolutely unstable — convection will develop given any trigger.
  • Conditional instability (DALR > ELR > SALR) is the most common case in convective forecasting; outcomes depend on whether parcels reach the LCL.
