Operational TAF Amendments & Edge Cases

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
  • Formulate appropriate change groups (BECMG, TEMPO) for deteriorating or improving conditions.
  • Apply the ICAO amendment-criteria thresholds (visibility, cloud, wind, phenomena) that force a TAF AMD.
  • Evaluate existing TAFs for ambiguity and propose operational amendments.
  • Discriminate between scenarios requiring TEMPO versus PROB groups.

Mastering Change Groups

The core of an effective TAF lies in accurately conveying changes over time. When conditions deviate from the prevailing forecast, forecasters use change groups: BECMG, TEMPO, and PROB. Applying these correctly ensures the TAF is not only accurate but also operationally actionable.

Change Group Definition & Duration Constraints Operational Implication
BECMG
(Becoming)		 A regular or slow transition to a new prevailing condition. The transition period should normally not exceed 2 hours (absolute max 4 hours). Establishes a permanent shift in the baseline flight category.
TEMPO
(Temporary)		 Fluctuations expected to last less than 1 hour in each instance, and in aggregate cover less than half of the defined time period. Pilots must carry alternate fuel to cover the TEMPO conditions. Very restrictive.
PROB30 / 40
(Probability)		 A 30% or 40% probability of occurrence. Used primarily for low-confidence events in the trend period (beyond 6 hours). Acts as a heads-up. Often treated similarly to TEMPO by dispatchers, so use it sparingly.
Avoid TEMPO Abuse
Do not string TEMPO groups back-to-back across long periods. If an intermittent condition will dominate the period (i.e., aggregate time is greater than half the period), it is no longer temporary—it is the prevailing condition. Use BECMG instead.

Amendment Criteria

Do not wait for the next scheduled TAF release if the current forecast is failing. Proactive amendments are a hallmark of a professional forecaster. You must amend the TAF when observed or expected conditions cross critical operational thresholds that are not accounted for in the current forecast.

When to Amend
Amend immediately when conditions cross between VFR, MVFR, IFR, or LIFR. Additionally, amend if the timing of a major frontal passage or thunderstorm onset changes by more than 2 hours.

The flight-category rule is the quick test, but ICAO Annex 3 also fixes the precise values at which an amendment is mandatory. The criteria are the same change thresholds used to issue a SPECI, and they are worth memorising:

ElementAmend when forecast/observed crosses…
Visibility150, 350, 600, 800, 1500, 3000 or 5000 m
Cloud base (BKN or OVC)100, 200, 500, 1000 or 1500 ft
Mean surface winddirection change ≥ 60° (mean ≥ 10 kt either side); or mean speed change ≥ 10 kt; or gusts change ≥ 10 kt (mean ≥ 15 kt)
Phenomena — onset or cessationTS, FZRA, FZDZ, FZFG, moderate/heavy precipitation (incl. showers), duststorm/sandstorm, and similar specified weather
Worked Example — deciding to amend
Your active TAF reads … 2412/2512 31010KT 9999 BKN030 … (VFR). At 1340Z the aerodrome reports visibility 2,500 m in mist with BKN006. Test the thresholds: visibility has crossed 5000 and 3000 m; the ceiling has crossed 1000 and 500 ft; and the field has dropped from VFR to IFR. Any one of those forces an amendment — issue a TAF AMD now rather than waiting for the next cycle, e.g. TAF AMD LLBG 241345Z 2413/2512 31010KT 2500 BR BKN006 BECMG 2415/2417 9999 NSW BKN025=, showing the expected recovery.

A common mistake is leaving a TAF "as is" because a previously forecasted PROB30 didn't materialize, or because a TEMPO condition lasted too long. If the conditions become the prevailing weather, you must amend to establish the new baseline.

Key Takeaways

  • Use BECMG for permanent transitions and TEMPO for brief fluctuations lasting less than an hour per instance.
  • If intermittent weather aggregates to more than half the forecast period, it must be promoted to the prevailing condition via BECMG.
  • Amend proactively. If the forecast crosses a flight category threshold unexpectedly, issue an amendment immediately rather than waiting for the next cycle.
  • The ICAO amendment thresholds are specific: visibility at 150/350/600/800/1500/3000/5000 m, cloud at 100/200/500/1000/1500 ft, defined wind changes, and the onset/cessation of specified phenomena.
  • Do not use PROB groups in the first 6 hours of the TAF. Make a definitive forecast for the critical period.
