Module 2 overview

In-flight Aviation Hazards

Estimated duration: ~100 min  |  5 lessons  |  Module quiz: 25 questions, pass at 80%

Comprehensive analysis of atmospheric hazards affecting flight safety, including turbulence, icing, convection, and visibility restrictions. Focuses on operational forecasting and risk assessment for aviation meteorologists.

By completing this module you will be certified to:
  • Identify and forecast various types of atmospheric turbulence and wind shear
  • Assess aircraft icing risk based on thermodynamic profiles and cloud characteristics
  • Predict convective development and associated severe weather hazards
  • Evaluate factors contributing to fog and haze for visibility forecasting
  • Determine cloud base and top altitudes for flight planning and terminal operations

Lessons

Lesson 1: Atmospheric Turbulence and Wind Shear ~25 min
  • Distinguish between mechanical, convective, and clear-air turbulence (CAT)
  • Identify synoptic and mesoscale features associated with severe mountain waves
  • Explain the formation and hazards of wake vortices
  • Forecast low-level wind shear and microburst conditions during takeoff and landing
Lesson 2: Aircraft Icing Hazards ~20 min
  • Explain the physics of supercooled large droplets (SLD) in icing formation
  • Differentiate between rime, glaze, and mixed icing based on temperature and drop size
  • Analyze tephigrams to identify layers of critical icing potential
  • Interpret WAFS icing potential products for flight path safety assessment
Lesson 3: Convective Hazards and CB Clouds ~15 min
  • Identify the life cycle stages of a Cumulonimbus (CB) cell
  • Describe the internal dynamics of severe convective updrafts and downdrafts
  • Predict secondary hazards such as lightning, hail, and squall lines
  • Evaluate convective potential using stability indices
Lesson 4: Visibility Restrictions: Fog and Haze ~25 min
  • Classify types of fog (radiation, advection, upslope) by formation mechanism
  • Identify the critical meteorological factors for fog onset and dissipation
  • Assess the impact of aerosol concentration and humidity on haze development
  • Interpret RVR (Runway Visual Range) trends in low-visibility conditions
Lesson 5: Cloud Base and Top Forecasting ~15 min
  • Estimate cloud base heights using temperature and dew point depressions
  • Determine cloud top altitudes from satellite imagery and sounding data
  • Forecast cloud coverage and layer thickness for aviation planning
  • Apply local topographical knowledge to sub-grid cloud formation
